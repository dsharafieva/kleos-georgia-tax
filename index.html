The system, in three charges

Almost nothing, if the status holds.

Georgia taxes a qualifying solo contractor at 1% of turnover, an unqualified one at a flat 20% of profit, and a company at 0% until it actually pays out. Contributions are voluntary for the self-employed, and VAT rarely reaches a contractor billing abroad.

1–20%

Income tax — from almost nothing to a fifth

Small Business Status charges 1% of turnover up to 500,000 GEL a year, 3% on the excess. Miss the eligibility bar — wrong activity, no status, revoked status — and it's a flat 20% on profit instead. A company pays 0% while profit stays inside it, 15% the moment it's distributed.

Contributions — voluntary, and usually skipped

Georgia runs a funded pension scheme, but it's mandatory only for employees with Georgian permanent residence. A foreign contractor on Small Business Status or a standard IE isn't required to join it — there's no ФСЗН-style floor sitting under the headline rate.

VAT — rarely a solo contractor's problem

Standard VAT is 18%, but registration only becomes mandatory once domestic taxable turnover passes 100,000 GEL in any rolling 12 months. Services billed to a client outside Georgia count as exports — zero-rated, and excluded from that threshold entirely.

A year in the life of a Georgian contractor's taxes

Day 1
Register, then apply for status

Individual Entrepreneur registration through a Public Service Hall (or by power of attorney) typically takes a single day. Small Business Status is a separate application to the Revenue Service — and since a 7 March 2026 rule change, it takes effect the same day you apply, not the first of the following month as before.

Monthly
Declare income, every month, even at zero

Small Business Status holders file an income declaration via rs.ge every month, whether or not they earned anything that month. Miss one and it's a fine; miss several and it invites a look at the status itself.

Watch continuously
Track where the work actually happens

The 1% only covers Georgian-source turnover — work done while physically in Georgia. Time spent working from elsewhere shifts that income out of the regime entirely, with its own tax question in wherever they actually were.

Since March–April 2026
The labour permit — check the exemption first

A March 2026 reform made a Special Labour Activity Permit mandatory for most foreign nationals doing paid work in Georgia, IEs included. A follow-up amendment six weeks later (Law №1509, 15 April 2026) exempted anyone working for a non-resident client on that client's business conducted outside Georgia — the standard cross-border contractor case. Confirm the exemption actually fits before assuming either way.

Also worth asking — but only your contractor can answer these:
  • Are they at or near the 500,000 GEL turnover ceiling this year, across all their clients — not just you? Cross it two years running and Small Business Status is revoked outright.
  • Have they kept up the monthly rs.ge filing, including in zero-income months? A missed filing can jeopardize the status before anything else goes wrong.
  • Do they have other income — rent, dividends, interest, royalties — mixed into what they report as business turnover? That was never covered by the 1%, and not separating it out is on them to fix.
  • Is real work happening while they're physically outside Georgia for stretches? That shifts income out of Georgian-source (and the 1%) and can change which labour-permit exemption applies.

Cases worth acting on

Three things here are yours to engage with — how you set the rate, how you scope the work, and whether the arrangement is even legal to run. A few other mechanics move the number too; they're the contractor's to manage, but worth understanding.

Which status they hold

Small Business Status (1–3% of turnover), a standard IE (20% of profit) and an LLC (0% retained, then 15%+5% on distribution) don't converge — the burden can run from roughly 1% to nearly 20% on the same income. Know which one you're pricing against before you agree a number, not after.

How you scope the work

Legal, financial, medical, architectural, audit and consulting work of any kind is excluded from Small Business Status by government resolution, however small the turnover. The line between "software development" and "consulting" often comes down to how the SOW is worded — a lever you actually hold.

The 2026 work-permit rule — and its own exemption

Since 1 March 2026, a foreign national doing paid work in Georgia generally needs a Special Labour Activity Permit. An amendment six weeks later (15 April 2026) exempted anyone working for a non-resident client on that client's business conducted outside Georgia — which covers most cross-border contractor arrangements, provided there's no local client or local footprint alongside it.

Also moves the number — but it's the contractor's mechanic to manage, not yours:
Where the work actually happens

The 1% only covers Georgian-source income — work performed while physically in Georgia. The same contract, worked from outside the country, is foreign-source and falls outside the regime entirely, with its own tax question wherever they were sitting.

Crossing the 500,000 GEL ceiling

Turnover above 500,000 GEL a year (~€167k) is taxed at 3% instead of 1%, on the excess only. Cross it two years running and Small Business Status is revoked outright from the following January — the fallback is the full 20%.

The 100,000 GEL VAT line

VAT registration triggers at 100,000 GEL of domestic turnover in any rolling 12 months — but billing a foreign client counts as an export, taxed at 0% and excluded from that threshold. Most single-client cross-border contractors never touch it.
