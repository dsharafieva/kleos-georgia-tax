Georgia · Contractor taxation guide · 2026

Hiring in Georgia?
Here's where the money goes — and how little of it is tax.

Georgia's Small Business Status lets a contractor pay just 1% on turnover — one of the lowest rates anywhere, which is why so many remote workers register here. But the 1% is conditional: it depends on the activity, a turnover ceiling, and where the work is actually done. Here's the money, and how to keep it that low.

The system, in three charges

A 1% headline, almost no contributions, VAT you can usually ignore.

Georgia's appeal is how little sits on top of the rate. Income tax can be 1%, there are effectively no social contributions on a solo contractor, and services sold to foreign clients fall outside VAT.

1% / 20%

Income tax — 1% if you qualify

Small Business Status charges 1% on turnover up to 500,000 GEL. Without it — or on income that doesn't qualify — you fall back to Georgia's flat 20% personal income tax on profit. There's no progressive ladder either way.

Social contributions — essentially none

There's no social tax on individual-entrepreneur income. A funded pension (2%) applies to Georgian citizens and residents in employment; a solo foreign contractor on the 1% regime generally sits outside it entirely.

VAT — but exports are zero-rated

Standard VAT is 18%, with mandatory registration over 100,000 GEL of turnover. But services supplied to foreign clients are generally zero-rated or outside scope — so a contractor billing abroad usually doesn't charge VAT at all.

A year in the life of a Georgian contractor's taxes

Day 1
Register as an IE, apply for Small Business Status

Register as an Individual Entrepreneur at the Revenue Service (possible remotely by power of attorney), then apply for Small Business Status. No Georgian residency or citizenship is required to register.

Monthly
File on rs.ge by the 15th, pay 1%

Declare turnover and pay the 1% on the Revenue Service portal every month — even in months with no income. Missing filings is a common way to jeopardise the status.

Watch continuously
Stay under €170k, on-list, and in-country

Keep turnover under 500,000 GEL, keep to permitted activities, and remember the 1% applies to Georgian-source income — work performed while you're physically in Georgia.

If you cross €34k
Consider VAT — though exports stay zero-rated

Turnover over 100,000 GEL triggers VAT registration, but services to foreign clients are generally zero-rated, so the practical VAT impact for a cross-border contractor is often nil.

The 1% is the whole point — and it's conditional.

Small Business Status isn't automatic or permanent. The Revenue Service can reclassify income to the standard 20% — or revoke the status entirely — if the conditions slip. For a contractor and the company relying on them, it's worth knowing what puts the 1% at risk before it becomes a back-tax bill.

Cases that change the final number

In Georgia the structure and a few conditions decide almost everything. These move the outcome most.

🎯

Which structure they register

Small Business (1%), standard IE (20% on profit) and an LLC (0% retained, ~19% distributed) don't come close to each other. Choosing on the 1% when you qualify is the single biggest lever on this page.

Huge swing
🚧

Crossing the €170k ceiling

Turnover over 500,000 GEL is taxed at 3% on the excess for that year — and exceeding it two years running revokes Small Business Status, dropping all income to the standard 20% from the next year.

Net ↓↓
🚫

Doing an excluded activity

Regulated and licensed fields — financial and currency dealing, legal, medical, audit, gambling and similar — are excluded from the 1% regime. Income from them is taxed at 20%, no matter your status.

Loses 1%
🔁

Reinvesting through an LLC

Under the Estonian model an LLC pays 0% on profit it keeps in the business — tax is only triggered on distribution (15% + 5%). For someone scaling and reinvesting rather than drawing everything, that deferral can beat even the 1%.

Defers tax
📍

Where the work is done

The 1% applies to Georgian-source income — work performed while physically in Georgia. Genuinely foreign-source personal income is untaxed, but claiming the 1% while working from elsewhere invites a source-and-residency challenge.

Micro Business Status

Turnover under 30,000 GEL (~€10k) a year, no employees, permitted activity — and the rate is 0%. It's the entry tier below Small Business Status, useful for a contractor just starting out.
